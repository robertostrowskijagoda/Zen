## 1. What Zen is

Zen is a free, peer-to-peer communication and community app. It has **no
central server and no operator**. Messages, posts and records travel directly
between users' devices over the Yggdrasil network. The author
(Robert Ostrowski-Jagoda, an individual developer) writes and publishes the
software; the author does not run a service, host your content, or control who
uses it.

Because of that, the author **cannot** read your content, identify users,
suspend accounts, remove content from other people's devices, or reverse
actions that happened on the network.

## 5. Alarm and safety features

The alarm, status, "I'm safe", date-safety and nearby features are **aids, not
emergency services**. They depend on a working phone, power, network or
Bluetooth, on friends being reachable and attentive, and on the operating
system allowing the app to run. **In an emergency call your local emergency
number (112 in the EU, 911 in the US) first.** Do not rely on Zen as your only
way to get help.

## 6. Moderation - community run

There is no central moderator. Safety in Zen comes from:

- **who you choose to connect with**: you decide who is your friend, which
  groups and pyramids you join, and you can remove friends, leave groups, block
  people and hide content;
- **local tools**: "Zgłoś" (report), "Usuń z mojego urządzenia" (remove from my
  device) and similar controls affect your device and what you pass on;
- **the pyramid**, where members can run their own rules, votes, bans,
  reports and appeals according to the structure's own constitution. These
  decisions are made **by the community members**, apply only inside that
  structure, and are not made or reviewed by the author. The author is not
  responsible for decisions of a pyramid, for its rules, or for any money-like
  points ("waluta") or balances inside it, which are not legal tender and are
  not redeemable by the author.

The author does not guarantee that harmful content will be found or removed.
Illegal content signed into append-only logs may remain on devices that already
hold it.

## 7. Law enforcement and legal requests

- The author does not hold user accounts, message content, contact lists,
  location data, IP logs or any other user data on Zen's behalf, because Zen has
  no servers. There is nothing to hand over beyond what is public on the
  project's GitHub page.
- The author **cannot** intercept, decrypt, trace or remove communication or
  content on the network, and cannot identify people from their Zen address.
- If a court or authority sends a valid request to the project, the author
  will respond within the law, in particular explaining the above. Requests
  may be sent through the GitHub project page (see the contact section of the
  Privacy Policy); do not send private data in a public issue.
- Reports of **illegal content** (for example child sexual abuse material)
  should go to the police or to the national hotline in your country (in
  Poland: Dyzurnet.pl; internationally: INHOPE). You can also tell the author
  via a private security advisory so that the project can consider
  safeguards, but the author cannot remove content from devices.
- People who find themselves under investigation should know that a
  device that holds data can be seized and read by authorities that have the
  right to; Zen's encryption is meant to protect data when your device is
  locked, not to hide wrongdoing.
